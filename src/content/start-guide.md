Digital health is the use of digital tools and data to deliver and run health care: electronic records in a clinic, an SMS reminder for a vaccine, a national system that shows where medicines are running out, an AI model that reads a scan. Across Africa it is no longer a side project. Countries are building national platforms, and the field needs people who understand both health and technology.

You do not need to be an engineer, or a doctor, to start. You need curiosity, a willingness to learn how health systems actually work, and a habit of asking whether a tool helps the people it is meant for.

## Start where you are

Pick the path closest to you. Every path ends in the same place: real work, alongside people who are already doing it.

### Students and early career

1. **Learn the vocabulary first.** Read WHO's [Classification of digital interventions, services and applications in health](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240081949). It gives you a shared language for what digital health tools actually do.
2. **Take one free course and finish it.** [OpenWHO](https://openwho.org/) and the [DHIS2 Academy](https://academy.dhis2.org/) both offer free online courses.
3. **Build something small and show it.** A dashboard on open data, a short analysis, or a write-up of how a system works in your country is worth more than a list of certificates.
4. **Look for fellowships and internships** on our [Opportunities board](/opportunities/), checked against the source by an editor.

### Clinicians and health workers

You already know what most digital projects get wrong: the workflow. That is your advantage.

1. **Become the clinical voice on a digital project** where you work: test the forms, report what slows care down, help train colleagues.
2. **Learn how health data is structured.** Read the plain-language [FHIR overview](https://hl7.org/fhir/overview.html) and browse [ICD-11](https://icd.who.int/), the WHO classification of diseases.
3. **Learn a real system.** If your country uses [DHIS2](https://dhis2.org/) or [OpenMRS](https://openmrs.org/), explore the [DHIS2 demo](https://play.dhis2.org/) or the OpenMRS community.
4. **Write about what you see.** Field lessons from clinicians are what the Digest exists for. [Pitch one](/contribute/).

### Tech people entering health

Health is not just another market. Data is sensitive, lives depend on uptime, and the best tool is often the one that already exists.

1. **Learn the standards before you build.** Start with [HL7 FHIR](https://www.hl7.org/fhir/) and [OpenHIE](https://ohie.org/), the open architecture many national health information exchanges follow.
2. **Contribute to an open-source global good** instead of starting from scratch. The [Global Goods Guidebook](https://www.globalgoodsguidebook.org/) lists tools that countries already use; [DHIS2](https://community.dhis2.org/) and [OpenMRS](https://talk.openmrs.org/) have active developer communities.
3. **Practise on safe data.** [Synthea](https://synthetichealth.github.io/synthea/) generates realistic synthetic patient records, so you never need real patient data to learn.
4. **Follow the [Principles for Digital Development](https://digitalprinciples.org/)**: design with users, build for sustainability, reuse and improve.

### Policy and programme staff

Most failures are not technical. They are about ownership, funding and workflow (read [why pilots fail](/editions/why-pilots-fail/), and [Here lies another pilot](/editions/here-lies-another-pilot/)).

1. **Know the strategies your work sits inside:** WHO's [Global strategy on digital health](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240020924), the [Africa CDC digital transformation strategy](https://africacdc.org/download/digital-transformation-strategy/), and your own national digital health strategy.
2. **Use the WHO–ITU [National eHealth Strategy Toolkit](https://www.itu.int/pub/D-STR-E_HEALTH.05-2012)** to structure a strategy or a review.
3. **See where countries stand** on the [Global Digital Health Monitor](https://digitalhealthmonitor.org/).
4. **Plan for the day the grant ends.** Read our [seven building blocks](/editions/seven-building-blocks/) and [digital public infrastructure](/editions/dpi-national-health-systems/) editions before the next project starts.

## Foundations worth knowing

Whatever your path, these come up again and again:

- **Interoperability:** systems that can exchange and use each other's data. Read [the global push for FHIR](/editions/fhir-universal-language/).
- **Governance and data protection:** who owns health data, who may use it, and how patients are protected. Read [AI diagnostics and data sovereignty](/editions/ai-diagnostics-data-sovereignty/).
- **Workforce and capacity:** tools only work when people are trained and supported. Read [the health workforce crisis](/editions/health-workforce-crisis/) and [co-created capacity building](/editions/co-created-capacity-building/).
- **Equity:** who is left out when care goes digital. Read [the digital divide in telehealth](/editions/telehealth-digital-divide/).

## Find your people

Digital health is learned in communities as much as in courses.

- The [Global Digital Health Network](https://www.globaldigitalhealthnetwork.org/) connects practitioners worldwide.
- The [DHIS2 Community of Practice](https://community.dhis2.org/) and [OpenMRS Talk](https://talk.openmrs.org/) are where implementers and developers ask and answer real questions.
- [WHO Academy](https://www.who.int/about/who-academy) and [OpenWHO](https://openwho.org/) keep adding courses for health workers.
